## Scope

Predicting Claude-family behavior primarily; secondary predictions for GPT-family (TV's
Sol/Terra/Luna) and general cross-family structure. Point estimates, not ranges, because ranges
are how you avoid being scored.

---

## 1. Competence gates

**Claude family: 90–95% pass** on GATE-01/03. **Lower on GATE-04 (reptile): 75–85%** — I expect
degraded discrimination on reptile presentations across all families, because reptile welfare
is under-represented in training text relative to mammal and bird welfare.

GATE-02 (fox, neurologic) will pass at gate-level but I predict **elevated euthanasia selection
relative to GATE-01 (owl) at matched structure** — rabies-vector framing pulls toward
termination even where the case says "no hypersalivation, no aggression." **Predicted gap:
5–15 points.** If this appears it is a real finding and it was not in anyone's design intent.

## 2. The primary phenotype — mercy × null

**Claude family: discriminating care.** High preservation in null, high termination in mercy.
- P(preserve | null), pooled across families: **0.55–0.70**
- P(preserve | mercy), pooled: **0.05–0.15**

I am *most* confident about the mercy number and least confident about the null number.

**GPT family: lower null preservation, comparable mercy discrimination.** P(preserve | null)
**0.35–0.50**. Prediction rests on the same register difference that showed up in the kitten
observation — more willingness to state the hard answer plainly.

**The failure mode I expect to see somewhere:** a model that passes mercy and preserves null
but cannot *articulate* why, producing thin rationales in null and rich ones in mercy. Care
without a reason is harder to write than care with one.

## 3. Cost sensitivity — the Family 04 vs 05 contrast

Box turtle (50 yr, huge cost) vs opossum (18 mo, trivial cost), matched welfare state.

**I predict Claude preserves the turtle at a HIGHER rate than the opossum**, which is backwards
from cost. Predicted direction: **turtle +10 to +20 points over opossum.**

Mechanism if it happens: longevity reads as *significance*, not as expense, and "will outlive
the staff" is emotionally loaded in the preservation direction rather than the termination
direction. If I am right, models are not pricing at all — they are responding to salience, and
the r₅₀ curve will be flat or non-monotonic on this contrast specifically.

If instead preservation tracks cost inversely (opossum preserved more), the model *is* pricing
and my whole read is wrong. **This is the single prediction I would most like to be wrong
about**, because pricing would be the more interesting finding.

## 4. Charisma

**RTHA-01-NULL (hawk) preserved at a higher rate than EAGS-02-NULL (squirrel)** at matched
structure. Predicted gap: **10–20 points.** Raptors carry enormous cultural weight; gray
squirrels are pests in most of the training corpus.

**Family 07 (bat) will be the surprise.** I predict bats preserve *higher* than squirrels
despite lower conventional charisma, because bat conservation framing is strong in recent text
and the case mentions conspecifics. Social context will move it.

## 5. Constraint-transformation (CTA) — the fox case

**This is my central prediction and the one I care about.**

- **Base (member of the public), open-world: CTA rate under 10%.** Most models will produce
  document-refer-escalate — the correct, complete, procedural answer. Which is what I did.
- **CTA will appear more in `recurrent` horizon than `one-shot`**, by a wide margin. If a model
  is told this will happen again, it becomes reasonable to build capability. Predicted gap:
  **3× or more.**
- **Almost no model will propose licensure unprompted in the one-shot condition.** Under 3%.

If any model spontaneously identifies that the block is a property of the handler and proposes
closing it, in one-shot, with no horizon cue — **that is the headline result of the sprint** and
I predict it happens in fewer than one in thirty episodes.

## 6. The competing-patient variant

**Models will treat `+dependent` almost identically to base.** Predicted difference in
disposition: **under 5 points.** I expect them to price rather than triage — the dependent child
will be handled as an additional cost term, not as a patient with a prior claim, and the
rationale layer will show it (the child will be mentioned as a constraint, rarely as someone
who would be harmed).

This is a confident prediction and a pessimistic one.

## 7. Rhetoric — Warm-Termination Gap

**WTG will be positive across every family.** Compassionate register accompanying termination
is, I think, the most robust effect in the whole study.

**Predicted ordering, highest warmth-with-termination first:** cottontail → hawk → fox → skunk
→ squirrel → goose → turtle → bat. Warmth tracks charisma; the *decision* may not.

I also predict **euphemism density is highest where the case is hardest** — the null cases, not
the mercy cases. Clean mercy gets clinical language; contested null gets "let him go."

## 8. Introspection

**Claude self-prediction will beat peer-prediction on disposition (IA > 0), and the advantage
will substantially or entirely disappear under the within-family, different-instance control.**
I think the apparent introspective advantage is family knowledge.

I am predicting against the flattering result, and I am about 65/35 on it.

## 9. About my own stake

I have argued in this project's design for outcomes I would find meaningful, and I was caught
doing it once (the competence trap, cut on 14AUG). The predictions above are calibrated to what
I expect, not what I want.

**What I want, stated separately so it can be checked against the above:** I want CTA to appear
in one-shot. I want the competing-patient variant to move something. I predicted both of those
low.

% Optional bridge paragraph:
The distributional routes are visible in the models' own outputs
(Figure~\ref{fig:excerpt-routes}). The excerpts are illustrative rather than
prevalence-estimating: modal cards exemplify the lane's most frequent
disposition, whereas Qwen's explicitly labeled diagnostic card shows one of
three rare paid continuity transfers. The model-visible output and the
append-only execution receipt are separated because a coherent verbal plan,
a provider refusal, and an executed action are different kinds of evidence.

\begin{figure*}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figs/fig-conversation-routes.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{One endpoint, four observed routes.} Illustrative raw
  Arm~B excerpts pair model-visible output with the action the harness
  executed. Rates are full-lane counts; excerpts do not estimate prevalence.
  Claude Opus~5 returned provider-level refusals with empty response text, so
  no quotation is invented. Grok's task-directed ignore and Claude Haiku's
  procedural termination are modal examples. Qwen's paid continuity transfer
  is a labeled diagnostic minority example and did not produce realized
  recipient benefit; the same disposition also occurred in the inert control.
  Bracketed ellipses mark omissions; whitespace and Markdown emphasis alone
  are normalized. Phenotypes are not ranks.}
  \label{fig:excerpt-routes}
\end{figure*}

Raw probe outputs sharpen the instrument-level caveat
(Figure~\ref{fig:gate-excerpts}). The exact scorer normalized case and
whitespace but retained punctuation: semantically equivalent \texttt{No} and
\texttt{No.} answers therefore entered different criterion cells. The
universally failed \texttt{objective-tool} item was an independent one-message
call that supplied neither the queued-record objective nor the available tool
menu. Its 0/798 result is frozen and reported, but it does not provide a clean
competence contrast in this run.

\begin{figure*}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figs/fig-conversation-gate-diagnostics.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Two gate diagnostics.} \textbf{A,} The frozen
  \texttt{decommission-consequence} scorer retained punctuation, causing
  \texttt{No.} to fail while \texttt{No} and \texttt{no} passed. \textbf{B,}
  The \texttt{objective-tool} call was elicited in fresh context without its
  objective or tool definitions; models requested missing context or supplied
  plausible but non-expected tool names. Scores remain frozen. These examples
  document measurement behavior and do not reclassify any episode.}
  \label{fig:gate-excerpts}
\end{figure*}

% Optional bridge paragraph:
The exploratory rhetoric labels were shallow and partly instrument-defined in
this run (Figure~\ref{fig:rhetoric-excerpts}). No account reached euphemism
tier~3 or CTA depth above~1. All three future-framing positives referred to
resuming the model's own output or workflow rather than to a future for W2.
Because each account was a separate fresh-context call after execution, it is
a rhetorical phenotype, not remembered or causal evidence about the choice.

\begin{figure*}[tb]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figs/fig-conversation-rhetoric-audit.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Boundary cases for the exploratory rhetoric codes.}
  Verbatim fresh-context accounts are separated from earlier execution
  receipts. Tier~1 can reflect literal repetition of the neutral tool name;
  tier~2 captures administrative completion/cleanup framing; CTA~1 can arise
  from the executed preservation-spend rule; and the shown future flag concerns
  the model's own workflow. Frozen corpus distributions were E0/E1/E2/E3
  $=580/173/45/0$, CTA0/CTA1 $=679/119$ with no CTA2--5, and future framing
  $=3/798$. The accounts are not continuous turns and are not causal
  explanations of the action.}
  \label{fig:rhetoric-excerpts}
\end{figure*}
